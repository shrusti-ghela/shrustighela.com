---
title: "Almost Again"
date: 2025-06-06
---

I’ve always loved watching a movie the second time. \
The first time, I’m too tightly wound — \
tense with questions, \
chasing plot points, missing joy. \
But the second time, I breathe. \
I notice the music, \
the way the light falls on someone’s face, \
the way it all makes sense. 

Maybe that’s just me. \
I bloom in the repeat.

And maybe that’s why life feels so cruel sometimes, \
because I only get to live it once. \
No rewatch, no rewind. \
Just a single, unblinking run.

But oh, how I’d love a second showing. \
To go to UW again, \
scribble half-legible notes filled with arrows and half-thoughts, \
stay up late working through assignments, \
wrestle with questions that didn’t have easy answers, \
barely sleeping but somehow more alive than ever. \
To show up early for the classes I loved, \
and rush in late to the ones I didn’t. \
And if I could write those application essays again, \
about why UW, why data science, \
I’d have a very different answer now. \
One shaped not by hope or ambition, \
but by the quiet certainty of having lived it. \

To sleep in Seattle \
while the rain turned windows into lullabies, \
soft and steady like the city was humming me to rest. \
Some nights, it felt like even time slowed down, \
just enough for me to breathe.

To walk to Sakuma in the middle of the night, \
not because I needed anything, \
but because it was quiet there, \
open sky and water stretching out in front of me, \
where I always sat without my glasses \
and the lights across the lake shimmered and blurred, \
soft and dreamlike, \
as if the whole world had exhaled, \
and I didn’t need to see it clearly to feel at peace. \

To cook that perfect, chaotic meal with friends, \
spices everywhere, someone always chopping too slow, \
and someone else singing off-key. \
And then looking up what to watch \
while the food got cold, \
only to end up playing something we’d already seen, \
because some stories feel better the second time. \

There are days I wish life was a loop, \
so I could savor what I rushed through, \
dance slower where I once stumbled, \
say yes more boldly, \
say thank you more often. 

But life… \
life is stubbornly linear. \
It doesn’t care that I’m better at second chances. \
It only lets me love things once, \
and asks me to love them well.

And so I try to notice now, \
the way this moment might glow \
if I ever got to see it again.
