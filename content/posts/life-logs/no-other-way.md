---
title: "No Other Way"
date: 2025-06-19
---

I was born in Palanpur, \
in a home made of cool marble and quiet love. \
It’s one of my earliest memories, \
not in sharp detail, but in feeling.

I remember the two little steps \
between our living room and dining room, \
the colored glass my maa had hand-painted with care, \
and the hush of the house — \
still, peaceful, safe.

Then the press closed. \
Pappa’s printing business folded, \
and we moved to Ahmedabad. \
He became a chemist, \
not by training, but by necessity. \
Opened a small medical store, \
and began working relentlessly.

From 8 AM to midnight, every day. \
Sundays were half-days, technically. \
But he stayed up late Saturday nights, \
sorting bills while the rest of the city slept, \
just to buy himself a little sleep the next morning, \
while Maa opened the shop. \
And if we were lucky, \
we got a few hours together as a family.

And when we did, \
we made the most of them.

Pappa loved movies. \
We went to late-night shows, \
often the last one of the day. \
There was barely a film we ever missed, \
we had to watch them all.

Maybe it was his escape, \
two hours where the world was simpler, \
the music told you how to feel, \
and everything, eventually, resolved. 

Afterward, we’d grab ice cream, \
talk about the film all the way home. \
Sometimes, even a late-night drive, \
no destination, \
just the city gliding past, \
quiet, ours. \
Love, in motion.

This rhythm became our life. \
After school, I’d sit at the shop, \
or wait at home while Hemin cooked food. \
The business expanded. \
He started new ones. \
Things got easier. \
Comfortable, even.

And then came 2020.

By then, Pappa had stepped back from the store. \
Delegated the day-to-day. \
He had earned that distance.

But when the pandemic hit, \
he returned. \
To the counter. \
To the calls. \
To the chaos. \
Not out of obligation, \
but because he couldn’t look away.

He worked like it was his duty to the city. \
To people. \
To something bigger than all of us.

That year, strangely, gave us more time together too. \
He came home early, \
around four in the evening, \
unheard of before. \
We played cards, \
watched movies at home, \
made popcorn, \
blended cold coffee in steel tumblers, \
and laughed in the soft glow of the TV. \
He returned to the kitchen after years. \
And I stood beside him, \
chopping vegetables, \
while the world gasped for air.

But every night, without fail, \
the phone would ring. \
Again and again.

“Do you have oxygen?” \
“Do you have this injection?” \
“Can you help?”

I asked him once \
“why not silence it? \
Put it on DND. \
Answer in the morning. \
Sleep.”

He said, \
“If I can help, they stop searching. \
If I can’t, they stop hoping. \
Either way, they need to know now.”

While others hoarded medicine, \
“just in case,” \
he refused. \
“These expire in weeks,” he said. \
“We don’t need them now. \
Someone else does.”

And while black market rates soared, \
he kept his prices fair. \
Made just enough to restock, \
never more than he needed. \
Not when the world was suffering.

Some would say \
he missed a great business opportunity. \
But did he?

That year, \
I saw what kind of man my pappa truly is. \
Not just a provider. \
Not just someone who works hard. \
But someone who, when the moment came, \
chose kindness over profit, \
clarity over panic, \
and people over fear.

Someone who didn’t do the right thing for applause, \
he did it because he couldn’t imagine \
doing anything else.

That’s the thing about ethics. \
They’re not supposed to change \
just because it’s hard.

And my pappa, \
he never named it ethics. \
He just lived it. \
Quietly. \
Consistently. \
Without ceremony.

No posts. \
No pride. \
Just presence.

Because for him, \
there was no other way. \
It wasn’t a philosophy. \
It was who he was. \
The simplest thing. \
The most unwavering thing.

And in a world that was unraveling, \
he held firm.

I always knew he was a good man. \
But during the pandemic, \
I saw his goodness tested, \
and I saw him pass, \
quietly, humbly, \
like it was the only way he knew how to be.

And I, \
who had always loved him, \
came to respect him \
in a way that went deeper than love.

I grew up beside him. \
But in that year, \
I saw him fully. 

Sometimes, \
you only meet people completely \
when the world begins to fall apart. \
Sometimes, \
that person is your father.
