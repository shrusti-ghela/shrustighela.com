---
title: "A Study in Pink"
date: 2025-06-17
---

I think about that night \
more often than I expect. \
Not because it was dramatic. \
Not because something big happened. \
But because something quietly shifted in me. \
The kind of shift you don’t notice until years later, \
when grief brushes past you in someone else’s story, \
or when a pink shirt in a crowd pulls your gaze \
and you don’t know why.

It comes back in fragments, \
the way memories do — \
a certain hush in the air, \
the sheen of rain on the road, \
and the kind of night \
that feels slower than the rest.

It had just stopped raining. \
The city was still. \
Washed and glistening. \
Puddles rippled under streetlights. \
The air smelled clean, new.

I was ten, maybe twelve. \
That age when love is a given, \
and rituals feel sacred \
simply because they happen every day.

Our ritual was simple: \
every night, no matter how tired he was, \
my Pappa took us out. \
A late-night drive. \
Ice cream. \
Or a movie that started just before midnight. \
Sometimes, just circling the neighborhood \
and coming home again.

That night, he’d taken the car — rain. \
So Maa and I decided \
to walk toward the main road to meet him.

The streets were oddly quiet. \
No usual hum of traffic. \
No people loitering. \
No vendors calling out. \
Just us, walking. \
Talking.

And then we saw the cart. \
The panipuri vendor’s cart, \
usually stationed across the road, \
had shifted to our side. \
Maybe because of the water.

At first, we didn’t notice anything unusual, \
until I did. \
A shape. \
A body. \
The man. \
Lying next to his cart. \
Still.

There was no one else around. \
I remember the way Maa’s hand \
stiffened around mine. \
We didn’t go too close. \
We weren’t sure if it was safe. \
Maybe he was asleep. \
Maybe he was drunk. \
Maybe.

A man passed by on the footpath. \
We called out, asked him to check. \
He leaned down. \
Then looked up sharply. \
“He’s bleeding,” he said.

Everything after that felt like muscle memory: \
calling the ambulance, \
my Maa’s voice calm but urgent, \
the first flicker of panic in my chest. \
my Pappa arrived just as the sirens did.

We followed the ambulance to the hospital. \
The doctors said it was a medical condition. \
He had fainted, collapsed, hit his head. \
Blood loss. \
The rain had kept the street empty. \
No one had noticed. \
Maybe no one would have.

And I’ve thought about that a lot since. \
What if the rain had lasted a little longer? \
What if we hadn’t decided to walk to the road that night? \
What if my Pappa had been early, \
and we had just reached the main road, \
slipped into the car, \
and driven off — \
never seeing the man lying there, \
never knowing? \
We came so close \
to a very different ending. \
One I don’t ever want to picture.

He made it. \
He was going to be okay. \
We offered to drop him home.

By the time we got there, \
his family had already been contacted. \
People were waiting. \
The quiet of the night had been replaced \
by murmurs, headlights, shuffling feet.

As we pulled up, \
his wife ran out. \
She cried. \
Hugged him. \
Hugged my parents.

Thanked them over and over again, \
her words frantic \
and full of relief.

I stood beside her, \
trying to feel the gravity of it all. \
I understood what had happened. \
I knew we had helped. \
I was glad.

But I wasn’t moved. \
Not yet.

And then his daughter came running. \
She was around my age. \
She didn’t say a word. \
Just threw her arms around her father, \
like she was trying to prove \
he was real.

She sobbed into his chest, \
holding onto him \
like the world had almost ended, \
and she hadn’t even known \
how close it came.

That’s when I saw it. \
The shirt. \
Light pink. \
Crinkled, stained. \
A deep red patch where his head had bled \
and dried.

Pink and red. \
Softness and injury. \
The almost and the aftermath. 

That was the moment \
something in me broke open. \
Because I saw myself in her. \
And I realized — \
this wasn’t a story I had witnessed. \
It was a story I could have lived.

And I cried then. \
For her. \
For him. \
For my Pappa standing beside me. \
For the fragile thread we all walk on, \
every day, \
without even knowing.

Now, when I see someone dressed in pink, \
I no longer see softness. \
I see that night. \
The quiet after rain. \
The man on the road. \
The daughter’s arms. \
And the weight of almost. \
What could have been. \
What nearly wasn’t.
