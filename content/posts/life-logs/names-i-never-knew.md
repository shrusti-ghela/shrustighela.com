---
title: "Names I Never Knew"
date: 2025-06-12
---

I wake in Houston, \
still blind without my glasses, \
eyes half-closed, \
still clinging to sleep \
and there it is: \
“Is your family okay?”

A message like a crack \
splitting the morning in two.

Fingers fumbling, \
heart ahead of breath, \
I call home. \
Maa, Pappa. \
their voices: steady. \
They’re fine. \
But a plane fell \
from the sky into Ahmedabad.

Into BJ Medical. \
Into a hostel where dreams slept. \
They say students may have died. \
Children of medicine. \
Children of hope.

Later, I call again. \
Maa says she knew someone. \
Knew. \
Past tense. \
A name turned into a silence.

My cousins — doctors, \
with ears to hospital corridors, \
speak in tongues of aftermath, \
“Pugilistic attitude.” \
I google it. \
And horror grips me \
by the throat.

I can’t work. \
I can’t think. \
I only imagine.

The woman, \
two years away from her husband, \
finally flying home. \
Gone.

The siblings, \
heading to start a business, \
building something bright. \
Gone.

A family, \
escorting a young student, \
chasing a future, \
now just a line in a headline. \
Gone.

And someone, \
just minutes into the drive back from the airport, \
having dropped off a mother bound for London — \
answers a call from her daughter, waiting across oceans: \
“They’re saying the flight crashed \
right after takeoff. \
Can you go check?”

What do you do with a sky \
that betrays?

There is only one survivor. \
One. \
How does he carry \
the silence of a hundred stories?

And how do his loved ones \
hold him now \
torn between miracle \
and mourning?

I keep refreshing the news, \
as if certainty will save me. \
As if somewhere, \
the ending might change.

But it doesn’t. \
It never does.

And still, \
I’ll hold space \
for names I never knew.
