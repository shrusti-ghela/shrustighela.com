My name is Shrusti (you can also call me Ghela). I am a Data Scientist and an [Independent Researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_scientist) (which, I’m told, is a pretty badass title).

Officially, I work at [WM](https://www.wm.com/) (formerly _Waste Management_) tackling [Routing Optimization problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_routing_problem). Outside of that, I spend most of my time thinking about how to better understand language models, so we build ones that are more trustworthy, transparent, and aligned with human needs. moving us toward a world of mutually-beneficial coexistence of humanity and AI.

The drive to understand systems and patterns has been with me since I was a kid. I was endlessly fascinated by puzzles, math, and the way things fit together. It’s this curiosity that pushed <verb> me to explore the depths of Information and Technology at the [Vellore Institute of Technology](https://vit.ac.in/). <something> I learned to combine my love for patterns with data. This curiosity carried me to the [University of Washington](https://www.washington.edu/), where I completed a master’s in [Data Science](https://www.washington.edu/datasciencemasters/). <ACTIVE_VOICE> 

During my time at UW, I worked as a Research Engineer at the [eScience Institute](https://escience.washington.edu/), collaborating with researchers on NLP problems. It was here that I realized I want to do more than just build systems. I want to ask questions that matter, define research problems, design experiments, explore the expected and the surprising, and when things went wrong, push further to figure out how to make them work. To test the waters, I took on a pilot project to get a real feel for what research actually is, which eventually led to [HALoGEN](https://halogen-hallucinations.github.io/).

Step by step, like [gradient descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient_descent) following the slope of curiosity, each experience has nudged me toward the questions I care about most today.
